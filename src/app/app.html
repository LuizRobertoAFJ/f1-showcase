HISTORY

The beginning of Formula 1 dates back to the 1950s, a period that marked the birth of modern motorsport. The first official Formula 1 World Championship took place in 1950, with its opening race held at Silverstone, England. During this era, legendary drivers such as Juan Manuel Fangio and Giuseppe Farina became pioneers of the sport, competing in powerful and dangerous cars. Although technology was still developing and safety measures were limited, Formula 1 quickly gained popularity around the world, establishing the foundations of the exciting and competitive sport we know today.

TODAY

In 2026, Formula 1 introduces a new generation of cars with smaller designs, active aerodynamics, and redesigned hybrid engines. Audi joins the grid after taking over Sauber, while Cadillac becomes the sport's 11th team, ending a period of 10 teams that had lasted since 2017. These changes bring new challenges for drivers and teams as they adapt to the updated regulations and compete throughout the season.
